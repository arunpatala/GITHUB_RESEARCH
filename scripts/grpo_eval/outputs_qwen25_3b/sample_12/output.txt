To determine how many years it will take before Carlos starts earning money from his lemon tree, we need to calculate when the total revenue from selling lemons exceeds the total expenses.

First, let's define the variables:
- \( C \) = Cost to plant the tree = $90
- \( E \) = Annual cost to water and feed the tree = $3
- \( R \) = Revenue per lemon = $1.5
- \( G \) = Number of lemons produced per year = 7

Next, we calculate the annual net profit (revenue minus expenses):
\[
\text{Annual Net Profit} = (\text{Revenue per lemon} \times \text{Number of lemons}) - \text{Annual cost to water and feed}
\]
\[
\text{Annual Net Profit} = (1.5 \times 7) - 3
\]
\[
\text{Annual Net Profit} = 10.5 - 3
\]
\[
\text{Annual Net Profit} = 7.5
\]

Now, we need to find out how many years it takes for the cumulative net profit to cover the initial cost of planting the tree. Let \( n \) be the number of years required.

The cumulative net profit after \( n \) years is given by:
\[
\text{Cumulative Net Profit} = n \times \text{Annual Net Profit}
\]
We want this to equal the initial cost:
\[
n \times 7.5 = 90
\]

Solving for \( n \):
\[
n = \frac{90}{7.5}
\]
\[
n = 12
\]

Therefore, it will take \(\boxed{12}\) years before Carlos starts earning money on the lemon tree.
