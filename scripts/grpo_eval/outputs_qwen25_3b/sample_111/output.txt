To determine how much water the boat had taken on by the time Julia reached the shore, we need to follow these steps:

1. **Calculate the total distance Julia needs to row:**
   Julia's shore is 64 seconds away.

2. **Determine the speed at which Julia rows:**
   Julia rows 20 feet in 16 seconds.
   \[
   \text{Speed} = \frac{20 \text{ feet}}{16 \text{ seconds}} = 1.25 \text{ feet per second}
   \]

3. **Calculate the total time in seconds it takes to row to the shore:**
   \[
   \text{Total time} = 64 \text{ seconds}
   \]

4. **Calculate the total distance Julia rows:**
   \[
   \text{Total distance} = \text{Speed} \times \text{Total time} = 1.25 \text{ feet/second} \times 64 \text{ seconds} = 80 \text{ feet}
   \]

5. **Determine the rate at which the boat takes on water:**
   The boat takes on 2 liters of water for every 10 feet rowed.

6. **Calculate the total amount of water taken on:**
   \[
   \text{Water taken on} = \left(\frac{2 \text{ liters}}{10 \text{ feet}}\right) \times 80 \text{ feet} = 0.2 \text{ liters/foot} \times 80 \text{ feet} = 16 \text{ liters}
   \]

Thus, the total amount of water the boat had taken on by the time Julia reached the shore is:
\boxed{16000}
