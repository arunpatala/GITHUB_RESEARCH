To determine the total length of all the baked goods Bill bakes in one day when put end to end, we need to calculate the total length for each type of baked good separately and then sum these lengths.

First, let's calculate the total length of the rolls:
- Bill bakes 300 rolls.
- Each roll is 4 inches long.

\[
\text{Total length of rolls} = 300 \text{ rolls} \times 4 \text{ inches/roll} = 1200 \text{ inches}
\]

Next, let's calculate the total length of the chocolate croissants:
- Bill bakes 120 chocolate croissants.
- Each croissant is 6 inches long.

\[
\text{Total length of croissants} = 120 \text{ croissants} \times 6 \text{ inches/croissant} = 720 \text{ inches}
\]

Then, let's calculate the total length of the baguettes:
- Bill bakes 60 baguettes.
- Each baguette is 2 feet long. Since there are 12 inches in a foot, each baguette is:

\[
2 \text{ feet/baguette} \times 12 \text{ inches/foot} = 24 \text{ inches/baguette}
\]

\[
\text{Total length of baguettes} = 60 \text{ baguettes} \times 24 \text{ inches/baguette} = 1440 \text{ inches}
\]

Now, we sum the total lengths of all the baked goods:
\[
\text{Total length} = 1200 \text{ inches} + 720 \text{ inches} + 1440 \text{ inches} = 3360 \text{ inches}
\]

Finally, we convert the total length from inches to feet (since there are 12 inches in a foot):

\[
\text{Total length in feet} = \frac{3360 \text{ inches}}{12 \text{ inches/foot}} = 280 \text{ feet}
\]

Thus, the total length of all the baked goods when put end to end is:

\boxed{280}
