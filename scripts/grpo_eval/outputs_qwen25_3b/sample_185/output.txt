To determine the total number of apples picked by Jenna and her mother, we start by identifying the number of apples each person picked.

First, we know that Jenna's mother picked 20 apples.
\[ \text{Apples picked by Jenna's mother} = 20 \]

Next, we are told that Jenna picked half as many apples as her mother. Therefore, we calculate the number of apples Jenna picked:
\[ \text{Apples picked by Jenna} = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{Apples picked by Jenna's mother} = \frac{1}{2} \times 20 = 10 \]

Now, to find the total number of apples picked by both Jenna and her mother, we add the number of apples each picked:
\[ \text{Total apples} = \text{Apples picked by Jenna's mother} + \text{Apples picked by Jenna} = 20 + 10 = 30 \]

Thus, the total number of apples picked by Jenna and her mother is:
\[
\boxed{30}
