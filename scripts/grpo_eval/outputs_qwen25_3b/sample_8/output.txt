To determine how far John is from home at the end of his 4-hour journey, we need to break down his trip into segments and calculate the distance traveled in each segment.

1. **Initial Drive:**
   - John drives for 3 hours at a speed of 60 mph.
   - Distance = Speed × Time = \(60 \text{ mph} \times 3 \text{ hours} = 180 \text{ miles}\).

2. **Traffic Jam:**
   - John spends the first 2 hours in traffic.
   - Distance = Speed × Time = \(0 \text{ mph} \times 2 \text{ hours} = 0 \text{ miles}\).

3. **Slow Drive:**
   - John drives for 0.5 hours at a speed of 30 mph.
   - Distance = Speed × Time = \(30 \text{ mph} \times 0.5 \text{ hours} = 15 \text{ miles}\).

4. **Fast Drive:**
   - John drives for the remaining time at a speed of 80 mph.
   - Total time left = 4 hours - (2 hours + 0.5 hours) = 1.5 hours.
   - Distance = Speed × Time = \(80 \text{ mph} \times 1.5 \text{ hours} = 120 \text{ miles}\).

Now, let's sum up the distances:
- Initial drive: 180 miles
- Traffic jam: 0 miles
- Slow drive: 15 miles
- Fast drive: 120 miles

Total distance traveled = \(180 + 0 + 15 + 120 = 315 \text{ miles}\).

Since John initially drove 180 miles away from home, the remaining distance to home is:
\[ 315 \text{ miles} - 180 \text{ miles} = 135 \text{ miles} \]

Thus, the final answer is:
\[
\boxed{135}
