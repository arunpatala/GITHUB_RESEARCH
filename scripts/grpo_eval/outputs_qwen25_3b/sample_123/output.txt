To solve this problem, we need to break it down into two parts: the distance Rosie covers during her first 3 hours of running and the distance she covers during the remaining time.

First, let's calculate the distance Rosie covers during the first 3 hours:
- Speed = 10 miles per hour
- Time = 3 hours

Using the formula for distance:
\[ \text{Distance} = \text{Speed} \times \text{Time} \]

For the first part:
\[ \text{Distance}_1 = 10 \, \text{miles/hour} \times 3 \, \text{hours} = 30 \, \text{miles} \]

Next, we need to determine how much time is left after the first 3 hours:
- Total time available = 7 hours
- Time already used = 3 hours

Remaining time:
\[ \text{Remaining time} = 7 \, \text{hours} - 3 \, \text{hours} = 4 \, \text{hours} \]

Now, we calculate the distance Rosie covers during the remaining 4 hours at a speed of 5 miles per hour:
- Speed = 5 miles per hour
- Time = 4 hours

Using the same distance formula:
\[ \text{Distance}_2 = 5 \, \text{miles/hour} \times 4 \, \text{hours} = 20 \, \text{miles} \]

Finally, we add the distances from both parts to find the total distance Rosie can run in 7 hours:
\[ \text{Total Distance} = \text{Distance}_1 + \text{Distance}_2 = 30 \, \text{miles} + 20 \, \text{miles} = 50 \, \text{miles} \]
