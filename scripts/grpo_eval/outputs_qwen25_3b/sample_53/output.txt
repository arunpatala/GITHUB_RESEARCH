To determine which day the mechanic earned more revenue, we need to calculate the total revenue for both Thursday and Friday.

**Thursday:**
- The mechanic repairs 6 truck tires.
- The mechanic repairs 4 car tires.

The cost for repairing truck tires:
\[ 6 \text{ truck tires} \times \$60/\text{truck tire} = 6 \times 60 = \$360 \]

The cost for repairing car tires:
\[ 4 \text{ car tires} \times \$40/\text{car tire} = 4 \times 40 = \$160 \]

Total revenue on Thursday:
\[ \$360 + \$160 = \$520 \]

**Friday:**
- The mechanic repairs 12 car tires.
- The mechanic does not repair any truck tires.

The cost for repairing car tires:
\[ 12 \text{ car tires} \times \$40/\text{car tire} = 12 \times 40 = \$480 \]

Total revenue on Friday:
\[ \$480 \]

Now, we compare the revenues from both days:
- Revenue on Thursday: \$520
- Revenue on Friday: \$480

The difference in revenue between Thursday and Friday:
\[ \$520 - \$480 = \$40 \]

Thus, the mechanic earned \$40 more on Thursday than on Friday. Therefore, the final answer is:
\[
\boxed{40}
\]
