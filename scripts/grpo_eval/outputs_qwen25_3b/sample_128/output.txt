To solve this problem, we need to determine the cost of each type of fruit and then calculate the total cost for the quantities Dillon bought.

1. **Cost of a Pepper:**
   Given that each pepper costs $15.

2. **Cost of a Watermelon:**
   The problem states that a watermelon costs three times what each pepper costs.
   \[
   \text{Cost of a watermelon} = 3 \times \text{Cost of a pepper} = 3 \times 15 = 45 \text{ dollars}
   \]

3. **Cost of an Orange:**
   The problem states that an orange costs 5 dollars less than what a watermelon costs.
   \[
   \text{Cost of an orange} = \text{Cost of a watermelon} - 5 = 45 - 5 = 40 \text{ dollars}
   \]

4. **Total Cost Calculation:**
   - Dillon buys 4 watermelons:
     \[
     \text{Total cost for watermelons} = 4 \times 45 = 180 \text{ dollars}
     \]
   - Dillon buys 20 peppers:
     \[
     \text{Total cost for peppers} = 20 \times 15 = 300 \text{ dollars}
     \]
   - Dillon buys 10 oranges:
     \[
     \text{Total cost for oranges} = 10 \times 40 = 400 \text{ dollars}
     \]

5. **Summing Up All Costs:**
   \[
   \text{Total cost} = \text{Total cost for watermelons} + \text{Total cost for peppers} + \text{Total cost for oranges}
   \]
   \[
   \text{Total cost} = 180 + 300 + 400 = 880 \text{ dollars}
   \]

Thus, the total amount of money Dillon will spend is \(\boxed{880}\).
