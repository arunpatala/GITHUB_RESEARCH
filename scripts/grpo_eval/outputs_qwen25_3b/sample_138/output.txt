To determine how many questions Mark left incomplete, we need to calculate the total number of questions he could have answered within the given time constraints for each test.

First, let's analyze the first test:
- The first test consists of 75 questions.
- Mark's rate of answering questions is 5 questions per hour.
- He had 8 hours to complete the test.

The total number of questions Mark could have answered in 8 hours is calculated as follows:
\[
\text{Total questions Mark could answer} = \text{Rate} \times \text{Time} = 5 \, \text{questions/hour} \times 8 \, \text{hours} = 40 \, \text{questions}
\]

Since the first test has 75 questions and Mark could only answer 40 questions in the given time, the number of questions left incomplete is:
\[
\text{Incomplete questions on the first test} = 75 - 40 = 35
\]

Next, let's analyze the second test:
- The second test consists of 100 questions.
- Mark's rate of answering questions is still 5 questions per hour.
- He had 6 hours to complete the test.

The total number of questions Mark could have answered in 6 hours is calculated as follows:
\[
\text{Total questions Mark could answer} = \text{Rate} \times \text{Time} = 5 \, \text{questions/hour} \times 6 \, \text{hours} = 30 \, \text{questions}
\]

Since the second test has 100 questions and Mark could only answer 30 questions in the given time, the number of questions left incomplete is:
\[
\text{Incomplete questions on the second test} = 100 - 30 = 70
\]

Finally, to find the total number of questions Mark left incomplete from both tests, we sum the incomplete questions from each test:
\[
\text{Total incomplete questions} = 35 + 70 = 105
\]

Thus, the number of questions Mark left incomplete is:
\boxed{105}
