To solve this problem, we need to calculate the probabilities of two events and then determine how much more likely one event is compared to the other.

### Step 1: Calculate the probability of rolling a number greater than 3

A six-sided die has faces numbered from 1 to 6. The numbers greater than 3 are 4, 5, and 6.

- Total possible outcomes when rolling a die: \(6\)
- Favorable outcomes for rolling a number greater than 3: \(4, 5, 6\) (3 outcomes)

The probability \(P(\text{number} > 3)\) is:
\[
P(\text{number} > 3) = \frac{\text{Number of favorable outcomes}}{\text{Total number of outcomes}} = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2}
\]

### Step 2: Calculate the probability of rolling two even numbers in a row

First, identify the even numbers on a six-sided die: 2, 4, and 6.

- Probability of rolling an even number on a single roll:
\[
P(\text{even}) = \frac{\text{Number of even outcomes}}{\text{Total number of outcomes}} = \frac{3}{6} = \frac{1}{2}
\]

Since each roll is independent, the probability of rolling two even numbers in a row is:
\[
P(\text{two even numbers in a row}) = P(\text{even}) \times P(\text{even}) = \left(\frac{1}{2}\right) \times \left(\frac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{1}{4}
\]

### Step 3: Determine how much more likely one event is compared to the other

We need to find the difference in probabilities and express it as a percentage.

- Probability of rolling a number greater than 3: \(\frac{1}{2}\)
- Probability of rolling two even numbers in a row: \(\frac{1}{4}\)

Difference in probabilities:
\[
\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{2}{4} - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{1}{4}
\]

Convert this difference to a percentage:
\[
\frac{1}{4} \times 100\% = 25\%
\]

Thus, rolling a number greater than 3 is \(25\%\) more likely than rolling two even numbers in a row. The final answer is:
\[
\boxed{25}
\]
