To solve this problem, we need to determine how far Dana can travel in six hours given her running, skipping, and walking speeds.

1. **Determine Dana's skipping speed:**
   - Given: Dana skips at 3 miles per hour.
   
2. **Determine Dana's running speed:**
   - Since Dana skips at half the speed she runs:
     \[
     \text{Skipping Speed} = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{Running Speed}
     \]
   - Solving for Running Speed:
     \[
     3 = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{Running Speed} \implies \text{Running Speed} = 3 \times 2 = 6 \text{ miles per hour}
     \]

3. **Determine the time spent running and walking:**
   - Total time available: 6 hours.
   - One-third of the time is spent running:
     \[
     \text{Time Running} = \frac{1}{3} \times 6 = 2 \text{ hours}
     \]
   - Two-thirds of the time is spent walking:
     \[
     \text{Time Walking} = \frac{2}{3} \times 6 = 4 \text{ hours}
     \]

4. **Calculate the distance traveled while running:**
   - Distance Running = Speed Running \(\times\) Time Running:
     \[
     \text{Distance Running} = 6 \text{ miles/hour} \times 2 \text{ hours} = 12 \text{ miles}
     \]

5. **Calculate the distance traveled while walking:**
   - Distance Walking = Speed Walking \(\times\) Time Walking:
     \[
     \text{Speed Walking} = \frac{\text{Speed Running}}{4} = \frac{6}{4} = 1.5 \text{ miles per hour}
     \]
     \[
     \text{Distance Walking} = 1.5 \text{ miles/hour} \times 4 \text{ hours} = 6 \text{ miles}
     \]

6. **Calculate the total distance traveled:**
   - Total Distance = Distance Running + Distance Walking:
     \[
     \text{Total Distance} = 12 \text{ miles} + 6 \text{ miles} = 18 \text{ miles}
     \]

Thus, the total distance Dana can travel in six hours is \(\boxed{18}\).
