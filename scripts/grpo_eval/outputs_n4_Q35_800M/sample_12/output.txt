To find out how many years it takes until the lemon tree earns money, we need to set up an equation for the Annual Net Profit.

**1. Identify the Costs and Revenue:**
*   **Cost to buy the tree:** $90
*   **Cost to water/feed per year:** $3
*   **Total Annual Cost:** $90 (purchase) + $3 (care) = $93
*   **Revenue per lemon:** $1.5
*   **Number of lemons/year:** 7

**2. Calculate Total Annual Net Revenue:**
The net revenue is the money earned from selling the lemons minus the money spent on the tree and care.
$$ \text{Revenue} = 7 \text{ lemons} \times \$1.5/\text{lemon} = \$10.50 $$
$$ \text{Net Revenue per year} = \$10.50 - \$93 = -\$82.50 $$

This negative number indicates that the tree incurs more expenses than it earns in a single year. The profit starts gaining when the net revenue becomes positive.

**3. Set Up the Profit Equation:**
Let $x$ be the number of years. The profit in year $x$ is:
$$ \text{Profit}_x = \text{Net Revenue per year} \times x + \text{Initial Investment} $$
$$ \text{Profit}_x = -82.50 \cdot x + 90 $$

For the tree to start earning money, the profit must be greater than or equal to zero at the critical year (the tipping point year). We solve for $x$ such that:
$$ -82.50x + 90 \ge 0 $$
$$ -82.50x \ge -90 $$

Dividing both sides by -82.50 reverses the inequality sign:
$$ x \le \frac{-90}{-82.50} $$
$$ x \le \frac{900}{825} $$

**4. Simplify the Inequality:**
Reduce the fraction by dividing numerator and denominator by their greatest common divisor.
Both are divisible by 25:
$$ x \le \frac{36}{33} $$
$$ x \le \frac{12}{11} $$
$$ x \le 1.0909... $$

Since $x$ represents the number of years, it must be a whole number (you cannot plant a lemon tree at half a year). The condition is satisfied when $x \le 1.0909...$.

This means the first year of planting will yield a profit (net revenue exceeds cost), but the second year will result in a loss.

**5. Conclusion:**
It takes **1** year for the tree to start producing profit.
