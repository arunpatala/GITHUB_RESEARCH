To determine how many miles Rosie can run in 7 hours, we need to break down her running into two segments: the first 3 hours and the remaining time.

1. **First Segment (First 3 Hours):**
   - Rosie runs at 10 miles per hour.
   - Distance covered in the first 3 hours:
     \[
     \text{Distance} = \text{Speed} \times \text{Time} = 10 \, \text{miles/hour} \times 3 \, \text{hours} = 30 \, \text{miles}
     \]

2. **Second Segment (Remaining Time):**
   - Total time is 7 hours, so the remaining time after the first 3 hours is:
     \[
     7 \, \text{hours} - 3 \, \text{hours} = 4 \, \text{hours}
     \]
   - Rosie runs at 5 miles per hour during this period.
   - Distance covered in the remaining 4 hours:
     \[
     \text{Distance} = \text{Speed} \times \text{Time} = 5 \, \text{miles/hour} \times 4 \, \text{hours} = 20 \, \text{miles}
     \]

3. **Total Distance Covered:**
   - Sum of distances from both segments:
     \[
     30 \, \text{miles} + 20 \, \text{miles} = 50 \, \text{miles}
     \]

Thus, the total distance Rosie can run in 7 hours is ####50####.
