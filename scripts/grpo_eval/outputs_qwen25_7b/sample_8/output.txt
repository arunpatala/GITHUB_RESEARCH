First, we calculate the distance John initially travels away from home. Using the formula \( \text{distance} = \text{speed} \times \text{time} \):

\[
\text{Distance away from home} = 60 \, \text{mph} \times 3 \, \text{hours} = 180 \, \text{miles}
\]

Next, we determine how far John travels back towards home during his return trip. He spends 2 hours in standstill traffic, so no distance is covered during this time.

Then, he drives for 0.5 hours (30 minutes) at 30 mph:

\[
\text{Distance covered in 0.5 hours} = 30 \, \text{mph} \times 0.5 \, \text{hours} = 15 \, \text{miles}
\]

After that, he drives for the remaining time of his 4-hour trip at 80 mph. The total time spent driving back is:

\[
4 \, \text{hours} - 2 \, \text{hours (standstill)} - 0.5 \, \text{hours (30 mph)} = 1.5 \, \text{hours}
\]

The distance covered in the remaining 1.5 hours at 80 mph is:

\[
\text{Distance covered in 1.5 hours} = 80 \, \text{mph} \times 1.5 \, \text{hours} = 120 \, \text{miles}
\]

Now, we sum up all the distances he has traveled towards home:

\[
\text{Total distance towards home} = 15 \, \text{miles} + 120 \, \text{miles} = 135 \, \text{miles}
\]

Finally, we find out how far John is from home by subtracting the total distance he has traveled towards home from the initial distance he was away from home:

\[
\text{Distance from home} = 180 \, \text{miles} - 135 \, \text{miles} = 45 \, \text{miles}
\]

Thus, the distance John is from home at the end of those 4 hours is:

#### 45
