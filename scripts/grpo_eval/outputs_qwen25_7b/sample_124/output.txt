To solve this problem, we need to determine how many letters were in the pile of already-stamped letters before Jennie started adding more.

1. First, we know that Jennie put stamps on one-third of the 60 letters needing stamps. So, we calculate one-third of 60:
   \[
   \frac{1}{3} \times 60 = 20
   \]
   This means Jennie added 20 stamps to the letters.

2. After adding these 20 stamps, the total number of letters in the already-stamped pile is given as 30. Therefore, the number of letters that were already in the pile before Jennie added the 20 stamps is:
   \[
   30 - 20 = 10
   \]

So, the number of letters in the pile of already-stamped letters when Jennie began was \boxed{10}. ####10
