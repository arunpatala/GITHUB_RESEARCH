To determine how many years it will take for Carlos to start earning money from his lemon tree, we need to calculate the net profit each year and find out when the cumulative profit equals or exceeds the initial cost of planting the tree.

First, let's break down the annual costs and revenues:
- **Annual revenue from selling lemons**: \(7 \text{ lemons/year} \times \$1.5/\text{lemon} = \$10.5/\text{year}\)
- **Annual cost to water and feed the tree**: \$3/year

The net profit per year is:
\[ \text{Net profit per year} = \text{Annual revenue} - \text{Annual cost} = \$10.5 - \$3 = \$7.5 \]

Next, we need to find out how many years it will take for the cumulative net profit to equal or exceed the initial cost of planting the tree, which is \$90.

Let \( n \) be the number of years required. The cumulative net profit after \( n \) years is:
\[ \text{Cumulative net profit} = 7.5n \]

We set this equal to the initial cost to find \( n \):
\[ 7.5n = 90 \]

Solving for \( n \):
\[ n = \frac{90}{7.5} = 12 \]

Therefore, it will take Carlos 12 years to start earning money on the lemon tree.
