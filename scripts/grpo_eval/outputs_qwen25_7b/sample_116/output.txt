To solve this problem, we need to use the given ratio and the total amount of sugar and water used.

The ratio of sugar to water is 7:13. This means for every 7 parts of sugar, there are 13 parts of water. Therefore, the total parts (sugar + water) in the mixture is 7 + 13 = 20 parts.

We know that Katy used a total of 120 teaspoons of sugar and water combined. Since these 120 teaspoons represent the total parts (20) in the ratio, we can find out how many teaspoons each part represents by dividing 120 by 20:

\[ \text{Teaspoons per part} = \frac{120}{20} = 6 \]

Since the sugar makes up 7 parts of the total, we can now calculate the number of teaspoons of sugar used:

\[ \text{Teaspoons of sugar} = 7 \times 6 = 42 \]
