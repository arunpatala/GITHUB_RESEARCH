To solve this problem, we need to determine Dana's walking and running speeds based on her skipping speed, then calculate the distances she covers while running and walking over the given time.

1. **Determine the running and walking speeds:**
   - Given that Dana skips at 3 miles per hour.
   - Her running speed is half of her skipping speed:
     \[
     \text{Running speed} = \frac{3}{2} = 1.5 \text{ miles per hour}
     \]
   - Her walking speed is one-fourth of her running speed (since she runs four times faster than she walks):
     \[
     \text{Walking speed} = \frac{1.5}{4} = 0.375 \text{ miles per hour}
     \]

2. **Calculate the time spent running and walking:**
   - Total time available: 6 hours.
   - Time spent running (one-third of total time):
     \[
     \text{Time running} = \frac{1}{3} \times 6 = 2 \text{ hours}
     \]
   - Time spent walking (two-thirds of total time):
     \[
     \text{Time walking} = \frac{2}{3} \times 6 = 4 \text{ hours}
     \]

3. **Calculate the distances covered while running and walking:**
   - Distance covered while running:
     \[
     \text{Distance running} = \text{Running speed} \times \text{Time running} = 1.5 \times 2 = 3 \text{ miles}
     \]
   - Distance covered while walking:
     \[
     \text{Distance walking} = \text{Walking speed} \times \text{Time walking} = 0.375 \times 4 = 1.5 \text{ miles}
     \]

4. **Sum the distances to find the total distance traveled:**
   \[
   \text{Total distance} = \text{Distance running} + \text{Distance walking} = 3 + 1.5 = 4.5 \text{ miles}
   \]
