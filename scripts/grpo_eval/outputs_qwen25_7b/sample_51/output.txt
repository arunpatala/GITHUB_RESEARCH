To solve this problem, we need to determine the distance Tom travels and then use that information to find out how long it takes for him to travel back.

\[ D = \text{speed} \times \text{time} \]

Substituting the given values:

\[ D = 10 \, \text{mph} \times 3 \, \text{hours} = 30 \, \text{miles} \]

Now, we know that the return trip is also 30 miles, but this time he travels at a speed of 6 miles per hour. To find out how long it takes for the return trip, we use the formula:

\[ \text{time} = \frac{\text{distance}}{\text{speed}} \]

Substituting the known values:

\[ \text{time} = \frac{30 \, \text{miles}}{6 \, \text{mph}} = 5 \, \text{hours} \]

Therefore, it takes Tom 5 hours to get back.
