First, let's break down the given information:

1. Sarah does 4 times as much laundry as David.
2. Raymond does half as much laundry as Sarah.
3. Sarah does 400 pounds of laundry.

We will start by calculating the amount of laundry David does.

From the fact that Sarah does 4 times as much laundry as David:
Let \( D \) represent the amount of laundry David does.
\[ S = 4D \]
Given \( S = 400 \):
\[ 4D = 400 \]
To find \( D \), solve for \( D \):
\[ D = \frac{400}{4} \]
\[ D = 100 \]
So, David does 100 pounds of laundry.

Next, we calculate the amount of laundry Raymond does. Raymond does half as much laundry as Sarah:
\[ R = \frac{S}{2} \]
Given \( S = 400 \):
\[ R = \frac{400}{2} \]
\[ R = 200 \]
So, Raymond does 200 pounds of laundry.

Now, we need to find the difference in the amount of laundry Raymond and David do:
\[ \text{Difference} = R - D \]
\[ \text{Difference} = 200 - 100 \]
