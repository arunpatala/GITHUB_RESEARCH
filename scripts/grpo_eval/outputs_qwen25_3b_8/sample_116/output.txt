To determine the number of teaspoonfuls of sugar Katy used, we start by understanding the given ratio of teaspoons of sugar to cups of water, which is 7:13. This means that for every 7 parts of sugar, there are 13 parts of water.

First, let's denote:
- The number of parts of sugar as \( S \),
- The number of parts of water as \( W \).

According to the ratio, we have:
\[ S : W = 7 : 13 \]

We also know the total number of parts (teaspoons and cups of water) is given as 120:

\[ S + W = 120 \]

Substituting the ratio into the total equation, we can represent the parts as multiples of some integer \( k \):
\[ S = 7k \]
\[ W = 13k \]

Now substituting these into the total equation:
\[ 7k + 13k = 120 \]

Combining the terms:
\[ 20k = 120 \]

Solving for \( k \):
\[ k = \frac{120}{20} = 6 \]

Now, the number of parts of sugar \( S \) can be calculated as:
\[ S = 7k = 7 \times 6 = 42 \]

Katy uses 42 teaspoonfuls of sugar. Therefore, the final answer is:
