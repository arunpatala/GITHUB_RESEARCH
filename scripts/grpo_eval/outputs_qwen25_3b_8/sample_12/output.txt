To determine how many years it will take before Carlos starts making a profit on his lemon tree, we need to:

1. Calculate the revenue from selling the lemons.
2. Determine the total yearly cost (planting cost + watering and feeding cost).
3. Find the point where revenue equals the total yearly cost.

First, let's establish the initial costs:
- The planting cost of the lemon tree: $90

Next, we calculate the revenue:
- Each year, the lemon tree produces 7 lemons.
- Each lemon sells at $1.5.
Thus, yearly revenue from lemons = \(7 \text{ lemons/year} \times \$1.5/\text{lemon} = \$10.5\).

The yearly cost to maintain the tree includes:
- The planting cost (which is already covered): This stays constant over time.
- Watering and feeding cost per year: $3.

The net expense per year (total yearly cost) is:
\[ \$3 \]

To determine the break-even point (the year in which revenues cover these expenses):
\[
\text{Yearly Revenue} \geq \text{Yearly Total Cost}
\]
\[
\$10.5 \geq \$3
\]
This inequality always holds true, meaning:
The net earnings (revenue minus cost) becomes positive immediately the following year after costs start offsetting the initial investment.

Since the question asks how many years Carlos must wait until he starts making a profit after all costs have been incurred at first, we note that he breaks even immediately after starting to earn from year 1.

- \(C_0\): Cost to plant the tree = \$90
- \(C_y\): Annual cost to water and feed the tree = \$3 per year
- \(I_y\): Income per year from selling lemons: \( \text{Number of lemons per year} \times \text{Price per lemon} = 7 \text{ lemons} \times \$1.5/\text{lemon} = \$10.5/\text{year} \)

Total cost to own and maintain the tree for \(y\) years includes:
\[ C_{total} = C_0 + C_yy = 90 + 3y \]

Total income generated by the lemon tree for \(y\) years is:
\[ I_{total} = I_y y = 10.5y \]

At the break-even point, the total cost equals the total income, therefore,
\[ 90 + 3y = 10.5y \]

Substitute and solve for \(y\):
\[
90 = 10.5y - 3y \\
90 = 7.5y \\
y = \frac{90}{7.5} \\
y = 12
\]

5. **Calculate Years Until Breakeven:**

To determine how many years it will take before Carlos starts earning money from the lemon tree, we need to find when the revenue surpasses the combined costs of the initial plant cost, yearly operating costs, and the fixed annual cost of providing 7 lemons.

Here are the detailed steps:

1. Initial cost to plant:
   \[
   \text{Initial Plant Cost} = \$90
   \]

2. Yearly costs: Costs include water/feeding ($3 per year) and providing 7 lemons (costing $1.5 each lemon).
   \[
   \text{Yearly Cost per Lemon} = \$1.5
   \]
   \[
   \text{Cost of providing 7 lemons} = 7 \times \$1.5 = \$10.5
   \]
   \[
   \text{Total Annual Operating Cost} = \$3 + \$10.5 = \$13.5
   \]

3. Net annual profit:
   \[
   \text{Revenue per Lemon} = \$1.5
   \]
   \[
   \text{Net Profit per Year} = \text{Revenue} - \text{Annual Cost} = 7 \times \$1.5 - \$13.5 = \$10.5 - \$13.5 = -\$3
   \]

Since net profit remains negative even over one year (which means his net cost exceeds his net income annually), it will never be profitable within one year.

4. To determine when the initial plant expense is covered by profit accumulation:
   \[
   \text{Initial Plant Cost} - \text{Cumulative Loss Per Year} = 90
   \]
   Since the net loss per year is $3:
   \[
   90 \div 3 = 30
   \]

Therefore, it will take \textcolor[rgb]{0,0.8,0} {30} years to cover the initial cost through accumulated lemon sales profit. Thus, your final answer is 30.
