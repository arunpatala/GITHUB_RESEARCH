To determine the price of the bag of marbles after 36 months, we need to understand that the price increases by 20% of the original price every two months.

First, let's define the price of the bag of marbles at the start (initial price):
- Initial price \( P_0 = \$20 \)

The price increases by 20% of the original price every two months. Therefore, the multiplier for each period (every two months) is:
- Multiplier = \(1 + 0.20 = 1.20\)

Next, we need to determine how many periods (each of two months) there are in 36 months:
- Number of periods = \( \frac{36 \text{ months}}{2 \text{ months/period}} = 18 \text{ periods} \)

The price after \(n\) periods can be calculated using the formula for exponential growth:
\[ P_n = P_0 \times (1.20)^n \]

Substituting the given values:
\[ P_{18} = 20 \times (1.20)^{18} \]

Now, let's calculate \((1.20)^{18}\):
\[ (1.20)^{18} \approx 39.366475 \]

Then multiply by the initial price:
\[ P_{18} \approx 20 \times 39.366475 = 787.3295 \]

Rounding to the nearest cent:
\[ P_{18} \approx 787.33 \]

Thus, the cost of the bag of marbles after 36 months is:
